Your audiobook server, native on the Mac.

Rapsod runs an audiobookshelf server from your menu bar, with no Docker, terminal or web browser, and gives you native apps to listen on Mac and iPhone.

Know before you use it.

Rapsod is built on audiobookshelf, which it bundles unmodified with Node.js and FFmpeg. A few things to know:

  • Polish for now. The interface is in Polish today. English is on the way.
  • The iPhone app is in development. Build it from source if you like; meanwhile, any audiobookshelf app works with Rapsod's server.
  • Your Mac is the server. Your books stay on it. Rapsod has no server of its own, and it is reachable from outside your home only if you set that up.

What it does

A server you don't have to set up, and a full native client on top of it.

A server without the setup

audiobookshelf with Node.js and FFmpeg inside the app, a library folder picked in a native window, start at login, and a supervisor that restarts the server and cleans up after it.

A native library

Shelves, a cover grid or a Finder-style list, series, authors, narrators, collections, playlists and listening stats, updated live.

Listening on the Mac

Chapters, speed, sleep timer, bookmarks, Now Playing and media keys, plus a menu bar panel with the current book.

Reading too

EPUB, PDF, comics, MOBI and AZW3, with your reading position shared with your phone.

Text that follows the narrator

When a book comes with its EPUB, speech recognition on your Mac maps every sentence to the recording. Follow along, tap a sentence to jump, or switch between reading and listening at the same spot.

Podcasts

Search Apple Podcasts, fyyd and Podcast Index at once, scheduled downloads, and sponsor segments marked and skipped, from chapters and feed data or, if you add one, an OpenAI-compatible model.

The whole server, managed natively

Libraries and scans, metadata matching (including LubimyCzytać, Audioteka, Storytel and Audible), covers, chapters, merging to M4B, users, backups, single sign-on and notifications.

An iPhone app of its own

Paired with the Mac by a QR code, every library merged into one, offline downloads, the synced text and an EPUB and PDF reader. In development.

At home or away

Sharing on your home network with Bonjour, and from anywhere through your own address or a Cloudflare Tunnel that the app runs for you.

Part of the system

Notifications, Shortcuts and Spotlight, and updates from GitHub, checked for Apple's notarization and a checksum before they install.

Native, not a web view

SwiftUI and Liquid Glass throughout, Swift 6 with strict concurrency, and no third-party Swift packages.

No web page to manage it.

Libraries, users, backups, API keys, single sign-on and notifications all live in a Mac settings window, and the changes save themselves.

The audiobookshelf web interface is still there if you want it, and every audiobookshelf app keeps working with your server.
